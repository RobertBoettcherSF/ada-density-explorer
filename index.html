2 · Packing is not weight

Pack the box

Density goes up if you add more pieces, or if each piece gets heavier, or if the box shrinks. Only the first and the third change how tightly the dots are packed.

Dots show the packing. The bar is the mass of one particle. The bar can grow while the dots stay put.

3 · Equal volume

Same size, different weight

Every cube has the same volume. The balance tips toward the greater mass. Changing the volume scales every cube together.

Tap a small cube to put it on the right. Gold border is left, teal border is right. The squares stay the same size.

4 · Many decades

Density ladder

The scale is logarithmic: each step of one is a factor of ten. A metric teaspoon is 5 mL everywhere on the ladder. Only the density changes, so the mass in that spoon changes.

Comparison masses are everyday round figures, except the proton, a 16-pound bowling ball, the Earth and the Sun. White-dwarf and neutron-star rungs are orders of magnitude. Interstellar space here is about one hydrogen atom per cubic centimetre.

Ticks mark the true place on the log line. Labels step apart when those places would sit on top of each other.

5 · Inside the atom

Mostly empty

A solid block looks full. Nearly all of its mass sits in nuclei. The picture uses the Bohr radius and the proton charge radius. An atom has no hard shell; this is a size comparison, not a drawing of an orbit.

ObjectAtomsNucleus

Nuclear density is 2.8×10¹⁷ kg/m³, the usual saturation density near 0.16 nucleons per fm³. The everyday density in the ratio is water. Both the Bohr radius and the proton radius are standard measured scales. The Earth mass and radius are the usual 5.9722×10²⁴ kg and 6371 km.

6 · Thought experiment

Imagine…

Not the same kind of measurement

The numbers in this section are order-of-magnitude figures, and they are approximate. They are here so the last question has something concrete beside it. They are not a claim about consciousness, and the question is not a result.

Neutrinos

Solar neutrinos at Earth are taken as 6.5×10¹⁰ per cm² per second, a round total in the neighbourhood of the standard solar model. The chance of an interaction on a trip through the Earth uses a sketch cross section of 10⁻⁴⁴ cm² and the nucleons along an Earth diameter. A different energy, or a different target, moves this by a large factor.

Dark matter, as astronomers sketch it

The usual local-halo estimate is about 0.3 GeV/cm³. Direct searches have not identified what it is. Nothing here treats that estimate as a weighed sample.

The dots are a picture of a stream, not one dot per neutrino. The dashed line is a path across the Earth.

A question, not a result

Could there be life we miss because we are dense?

Some people who describe a near-death experience speak of a living presence that was vivid to them and easy to miss from ordinary waking life. Nothing on this page tests those accounts.

What the figures do show is narrower. Neutrinos already pass through a person who feels nothing. The dark-matter sketch, if it is right, says a kind of mass can share our space without sharing the way atoms touch. Our own matter is, by comparison, tightly packed and strongly interacting.

Could there be life that moves through our world as lightly as a neutrino moves through the Earth — life we do not perceive because we are, comparatively, dense? That is a question to wonder about. It is not a claim this explorer can weigh.

Sources for the constants
  • Standard gravity 9.80665 m/s², the conventional value.
  • Pure metals at room temperature, from standard physical tables: aluminium 2.70, iron 7.874, lead 11.34, gold 19.32, osmium 22.59 g/cm³.
  • Ice 917 kg/m³ and fresh water 1000 kg/m³ are the conventional teaching values. Ice Ih near 0 °C is about 917. Liquid water near 4 °C is 999.97.
  • Ethanol 789 kg/m³ is about 789.3 at 20 °C. Mercury 13534 kg/m³ is about 13.534 g/cm³ near 25 °C.
  • Sea water 1025, olive oil 910, honey 1420, cork 240, pine 500 and granite 2700 are typical values. They vary.
  • Air 1.225 kg/m³ is the International Standard Atmosphere at sea level.
  • Interstellar space on the ladder is one proton mass per cm³, an order of magnitude. A white dwarf is shown as 10⁹ kg/m³, also an order of magnitude.
  • Nuclear density 2.8×10¹⁷ kg/m³ is the usual saturation density. The Bohr radius is the CODATA value. The proton radius 0.8414 fm is the CODATA 2018 charge radius.
  • Earth: 5.9722×10²⁴ kg and a volumetric mean radius of 6371 km. Sun: 1.9885×10³⁰ kg.
  • Neutrino flux 6.5×10¹⁰ cm⁻² s⁻¹ and cross section 10⁻⁴⁴ cm² are an order-of-magnitude solar-neutrino sketch. Local dark matter is the usual 0.3 GeV/cm³ halo estimate.
